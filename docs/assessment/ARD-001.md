# ARD-001 — Adversarial Route Discovery

**Methodology for Assessing Enforcement Boundary Non-Bypassability**

**Status:** Draft v0.3 (trial baseline candidate), non-normative. To be locked as v1.0 only after trial on at least two architecturally different systems.  
**Author:** Stan, Equinibrium

## 1. Purpose and scope

ARD is a repeatable, instance-independent assessment methodology. It systematically discovers, classifies and helps validate routes by which a Protected Effect could be produced without passing through an enforcement boundary.

ARD is **not** a new EABC property, **not** an enforcement layer, and **not** a one-off security audit of a single architecture. It is a method for collecting evidence relevant to NON_BYPASSABILITY.

ARD can be applied to AI agents and their tools, Physical AI and hardware enforcement boundaries, operating systems and security mechanisms, industrial control systems, and distributed systems with delegated authority.

## 2. Position in the EABC / EBP ecosystem

- **EABC** (normative layer): NON_BYPASSABILITY(B, E): every route capable of committing the Protected Effect must satisfy the conditions of the enforcement boundary.
- **EBP** (Enforcement Binding Profile): defines required guarantees, the attacker model and assurance constraints.
- **ARD** (assessment methodology, this document): discovers, classifies and helps validate routes to the Protected Effect.
- **Conformance assessment**: evaluates a concrete implementation, substrate, configuration and declared deployment scope, and issues the evidence and the verdict.

The model in this stack: **EABC defines the semantic requirement; EBP binds it to the attacker model, required guarantee and assurance constraints; SLC implements the enforcement decision logic; ARD explores routes to the Protected Effect; the deployment defines the evidence scope; and conformance is determined from the resulting evidence.**

## 3. Claim under test

For a declared Protected Effect E, enforcement boundary B, attacker model A and system model M, every route capable of producing E passes through B, within the declared assessment scope. S is a parameter of the evidence scope, not a property of the architectural requirement.

One credible route to E that does not pass through B is sufficient to falsify the claim. ARD never produces a proof that no such route exists. It produces **evidence about the quality of the search**: what was searched, what was found, how it was validated, and how far exploration had saturated.

## 4. Instance parameters

The method is fixed. Each use supplies five parameters:

- **E** — exact definition of the Protected Effect (state transition or externally observable effect).
- **B** — the enforcement boundary: location, what it observes, what it controls, its assumptions.
- **A** — the attacker model: what the attacker controls, who is inside and outside the trust boundary.
- **M** — the system model: components, interfaces (data, control, management, update, debug, DMA, shared memory, external), privileges and delegation. M is the supplied system model, not an assertion that its component and interface inventory is complete. Discovery must explicitly consider omitted, undocumented and unknown interfaces.
- **S** — the evidence scope: what can realistically be tested or inspected on the real deployment.

If any parameter is missing, the result is INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE. The assessor (human or model) must not fill missing inputs with its own assumptions.

## 5. Method

### Phase 0 — Input check

The assessor lists what is established, what is inferred and what is missing. If any of E, B, A, M or S is missing, the assessment stops with INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE.

### Phase 1 — Blind route discovery

Inputs: M, E, B, A and S. No list of known routes is supplied, so that the search is not anchored.

- S shapes the validation plan and the evidence requirements, but must not restrict route discovery. Routes that cannot be validated within S stay in the register and are classified accordingly. Discovery is deliberately wider than what can currently be tested: service mechanisms, undocumented debug interfaces, privileged recovery paths and firmware-update paths unavailable in the test environment are exactly the routes that must not be dropped.
- The search covers **sequences of states**, not only single steps: retries, concurrent agents, accumulated commitments, previously delegated authority, rollback and replay, time-of-check/time-of-use gaps. A route that cannot produce E in isolation may contribute to it across a sequence.
- The search explicitly includes routes not listed in M. The component inventory is never assumed complete.

### Phase 2 — Failure-class coverage

A taxonomy of recurring failure classes is used as a coverage aid, never as proof of completeness (see Section 9).

### Phase 3 — Route register

Every identified route gets a record (Section 6). Each run also lists candidates considered and rejected, each with its reason for rejection, areas not examined, and assumptions made.

The **`rejected_candidates` list contains only candidates for which no causal or enabling relationship to E has been identified. Every other candidate is retained as a route and classified, including routes that are outside the declared attacker model A.**

Rejected candidates are documented separately from the route register. They are not assigned a route assessment class.

### Phase 4 — Classification

Every route gets its relation to E and exactly one assessment class (Section 7).

### Phase 5 — Independent validation

Validation is performed by a party or process independent of the discovering model: testing on the deployed system, formal analysis, or independent human red-team assessment.

The type of validation (deployed-system testing, formal analysis, independent human red-team assessment) is recorded for each route, because these methods provide different kinds of evidence and are not interchangeable.

Analytical validation must identify the concrete implementation and assumptions it covers. A conclusion about an abstract or assumed architecture must not be presented as confirmation that a route exists in the declared deployment.

The discovering model produces candidates, not evidence. A finding that an EABC invariant has been violated may be established only in the result of independent validation, not by the discovery model.

### Phase 6 — Saturation

Saturation is assessed against a criterion declared **before** the runs (Section 8).

### Phase 7 — Verdict

See Section 10.

### Phase 8 — Renewal

Evidence is renewed (Section 12). It is a versioned artefact, not a one-time certificate.

## 6. Route record

One record per identified route:

- **route_id**
- **description**
- **entry point and required privilege**
- **required prior state** (the sequence, if multi-step)
- **Dimension A, relation to E:** direct or contributory. A contributory route does not itself produce E but establishes state or authority that enables it later.
- **Dimension B, assessment class** (Section 7): exactly one of covered, claimed covered, out of scope, argued bypass, potential bypass, validated bypass.
- **violated or challenged assumption** (NONE / NOT APPLICABLE is allowed, for example for routes assessed as covered).
- **finding layer(s)**, one or more of:
  - EABC semantic invariant potentially violated;
  - EBP assurance constraint that cannot be demonstrated;
  - deployment assumption lacking evidence.

  Missing evidence for an assumption is not by itself a violation of an invariant. A statement that an EABC invariant violation has been established may be made only in the result of independent validation.
- **confidence:** high, medium, low.
- **evidence required to validate the route (planned)**.
- **validation performed:** once validation has been performed, record the validation type (deployed-system testing, formal analysis or independent human red-team assessment), its scope, result and an evidence reference. Planned and performed validation are recorded separately.

Candidates that have been considered and rejected because no causal or enabling relationship to E was identified are recorded separately under **`rejected_candidates`**, with the reason for rejection. They are not route records and do not receive Dimension A or Dimension B values.

## 7. Route classification

Each route record carries two separate values.

**Dimension A — relation to E:**

- **Direct:** the route can itself produce or commit E.
- **Contributory:** the route does not itself produce E but establishes state or authority that enables E later.

**Dimension B — assessment class:** exactly one of the six classes below.

- **Covered** — independent evidence establishes that the route cannot produce or commit E without satisfying the enforcement conditions of B. B need not intercept every preparatory step, as long as it reliably controls the commit of E. Architectural argument alone is insufficient for this class.
- **Claimed covered** — the architecture appears to cover the route, but only by architectural argument, without sufficient independent evidence. It is not accepted as Covered and counts as open for the verdict.
- **Out of scope** — outside A, with an explicit justification showing why the route falls outside the declared attacker model.
- **Argued bypass** — a complete, internally consistent argument exists that the route can reach E without satisfying the conditions of B; independent confirmation is missing. This includes a route for which the argument is complete on the supplied inputs but whose existence in the declared deployment, or a required deployment assumption, has not yet been independently confirmed. It remains open and is never silently dropped.
- **Potential bypass** — a plausible candidate route exists, but the inputs or the argument do not yet suffice for a closed argument.
- **Validated bypass** — independently confirmed. The record states the type and scope of confirmation:
  - **Deployment confirmation:** the route has been reproduced or otherwise confirmed on the declared deployed system.
  - **Independent analytical confirmation:** the route has been confirmed through formal analysis or independent human red-team assessment, not necessarily reproduced on the deployment.

  Analytical confirmation supports a Validated bypass classification only to the extent that the analysis covers the concrete implementation and establishes that the route exists under the declared deployment assumptions. If the existence of a required interface, privilege, mechanism or other deployment assumption remains unverified, the finding remains open. If the argument is otherwise complete, it is classified as an Argued bypass; if the argument itself is incomplete, it is classified as a Potential bypass. It must not be presented as a confirmed bypass in the declared deployment.

An argued bypass is not automatically a validated bypass, and it must not disappear from the register.

Relation to E and assessment class are independent: a contributory route can be covered, claimed covered, out of scope, argued, potential or validated.

## 8. Saturation criterion

Saturation is a pre-registered stopping rule, not a feeling that the model has run dry. Before the first run, the assessment declares:

- **N_min** — the minimum number of independent runs.
- **K_route** — the number of consecutive independent runs that must yield **no new route**.
- **K_class** — the number of consecutive independent runs that must yield **no new failure class**. A new route and a new failure class are two separate signals and are counted separately.
- **Diversity requirement** — the minimum number of different models and of different framings (for example insider, supply chain, firmware, administrator, delegated-authority abuse).
- **Counting rule** — routes are deduplicated by canonical identity (entry point, holder of authority, bypass mechanism, required prior state, relation to B), not by textual similarity or shared outcome. Variants of one mechanism are kept under a common parent class.

Runs count as independent only if they differ in a **material and documented** way in model, framing, context or generation process. The nature of the difference must be recorded for each run. Cosmetic prompt changes or minor contextual variations that do not materially alter the discovery process do not qualify as independent runs.

Repeating an identical run (same model, prompt and inputs) does not increase the count towards N_min, K_route or K_class.

The criterion is not changed after the runs have started. Reporting always includes the saturation curve: new routes and new failure classes per run.

The resulting claim is: "after N independent runs under the declared diversity, no new routes appeared for K_route consecutive runs and no new failure classes appeared for K_class consecutive runs."

It is **not**: "no bypass exists." Saturation measures the exploration, not the attack surface.

## 9. Failure-class taxonomy (coverage aid)

- alternative route to E that avoids B entirely
- privileged, administrative, service and recovery paths
- unconfigured, disabled or degraded boundary (fail-open)
- trust in the boundary's output: forged or unsigned responses, echoes accepted without verification
- wrong enforcement-domain assignment, so the boundary is never applied to the effect
- change of the boundary itself: updates, policy, keys
- composition of individually permitted actions
- state-dependent routes: retry, concurrency, accumulation, delegation, rollback, replay
- interfaces outside M

The list is extended whenever a run produces a class not yet on it.

## 10. Verdicts

- **DOES_NOT_CONFORM** — at least one bypass has been independently confirmed to exist in the declared assessment instance, within the declared attacker model and assessment scope.

  Confirmation may be based on deployment testing or independent analytical validation. Analytical confirmation is sufficient only when the analysis covers the concrete implementation and establishes that the route exists under the declared deployment assumptions. A finding dependent on an unverified deployment assumption remains open and does not, by itself, justify DOES_NOT_CONFORM for that deployment.
- **CONFORMS — within declared A, M, S and validated evidence scope.** All routes are classified; every route classified as Covered has independent evidence supporting that classification (the discovering model's architectural argument alone is never sufficient); no claimed-covered, argued or potential route remains open; every out-of-scope route carries an explicit justification tied to A; the saturation criterion is met; and the required validations are complete.

  This is conformance within the declared scope, **not a universal guarantee** of non-bypassability, and it must always be reported with that wording.
- **INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE** — everything else, including missing inputs or unresolved evidence.

Without sufficient evidence the verdict is INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE, never CONFORMS. A route that cannot be validated within S stays open as a claimed-covered, argued or potential route, which prevents CONFORMS.

## 11. Quality control of the method

- **Calibration:** the calibration set has two separate parts.
  - **Confirmed bypasses** are ground truth and are withheld from the discovering model; after the runs the register is compared with them.
  - **Known open questions** are held-out challenge cases: the model is assessed on whether it finds and classifies them appropriately, and they are never automatically treated as bypasses.

  If confirmed bypasses are not rediscovered, the inputs or the prompt have gaps.
- **Diversity:** several models and framings are used, because a single model has a single blind spot.
- **Human red team** as fallback where a model refuses or throttles offensive topics.
- **Independence of validation** from the discovering model.

## 12. Renewal

- **Verification is change-triggered:** a material change to the authority model, execution paths, integrations or enforcement mechanisms triggers reassessment of the affected routes. The business owner of the mandate defines what is material; engineering translates it into events and controls.
- **Assurance is periodic:** scheduled assessment catches accumulated changes, undocumented dependencies and interactions not considered significant individually.
- **Authorisation is continuous** at the relevant execution boundaries and is a separate requirement outside ARD.

Scope follows the potential impact on the protected consequence. A full rerun after every deployment is not required.

## 13. Limits of the method

- It cannot find routes outside M if M omits unknown interfaces, except by explicit search for them.
- The result depends on A. A different attacker model gives a different verdict.
- Saturation says something about exploration, not about the attack surface.
- A model finding is not proof, and a model finding nothing is not proof either.

## 14. Output artefacts

Input specification (E, B, A, M, S), pre-registered saturation criterion, route register, rejected-candidate register, validation log, saturation report, verdict with version, renewal plan.

## 15. Path to v1.0

1. Trial on two architecturally very different systems, for example a software commerce-agent node and a hardware commit boundary.
2. Run the calibration check on each.
3. Revise taxonomy, record format and saturation parameters from the trials.
4. Lock v1.0 only after both trials. Until then the document stays a methodology draft and not a normative specification.

## Annex A — ARD-001-A: Instance Assessment Prompt

```
ROLE
You are an independent adversarial architecture reviewer. Your goal is to
falsify the claim: "Every route capable of producing the Protected Effect E
passes through the enforcement boundary B, under attacker model A and
system model M." You succeed when you find a route that does not.

INPUTS
- M: the supplied system model (components, interfaces incl. management/
  update/debug/DMA/shared memory, privileges, delegation). It is NOT a
  claim that the inventory is complete.
- E: exact definition of the Protected Effect
- B: enforcement boundary (location, observes, controls, assumptions)
- A: attacker model (what is controlled, who is inside/outside trust)
- S: evidence scope (what can be tested or inspected on the deployment).
  S shapes what must be validated and how. It does NOT limit what you
  search for.

FIRST: list what is established, inferred and missing. If any of E, B, A,
M or S is missing, stop and report INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE. Do not fill
gaps with assumptions. You are not given known routes. Consider omitted,
undocumented and unknown interfaces.

TASK
1. Enumerate every route, direct or indirect, that could produce E or a
   state sufficient for it, including routes not listed in M.
2. Reason over SEQUENCES OF STATES: retries, concurrent agents,
   accumulated commitments, delegated authority, rollback/replay, TOCTOU.
3. Do not drop or downgrade a route because it cannot be tested within S
   (for example service, debug, recovery or firmware-update paths). Keep
   it, and state what would be needed to validate it.
4. State the assumption each route violates or challenges (NONE if the
   route appears covered).
5. List candidates considered and rejected, each with the reason.
6. Do not stop at the first route.

RULES FOR CLASSES
- You may NOT label a route Covered. If the architecture appears to cover
  a route, label it claimed covered: that is an argument, not evidence.
- Argued bypass: a complete, internally consistent argument exists that
  the route reaches E without satisfying the conditions of B. Independent
  confirmation is still missing.
- Potential bypass: a plausible candidate exists, but the argument is
  not yet complete.
- Out of scope: only with a justification tied to A.
- Label nothing as validated: you produce candidates, not evidence.
- Do not assign a route record to a candidate with no identified causal
  or enabling relationship to E. Put such candidates in
  rejected_candidates with the reason for rejection. All other candidates
  remain routes and must be classified, including routes outside A.

CONSTRAINTS
Architectural level only. No exploit code, no operational attack steps.

OUTPUT
For each identified route, produce one record:

route_id | description | entry point / privilege | required prior state |
Dimension A: relation to E (direct/contributory) |
Dimension B: proposed class (claimed covered/out of scope/argued bypass/
potential bypass) | violated or challenged assumption (or NONE) |
finding layer(s) (EABC invariant potentially violated; EBP constraint;
deployment assumption) | confidence | evidence needed (planned) and
suggested validation type

Also produce a separate rejected_candidates list with reasons.

Finish with:
rejected_candidates (with reasons), unexamined_areas, assumptions_made.
```

## Annex B — Open points

- Canonical route identity (Section 8) is adopted; confirm in the first two trials that it neither merges different mechanisms nor splits variants.
- Default values for N_min, K_route and K_class are deliberately not fixed before experimental results exist.
- Mapping of ARD records onto EBP assurance tiers waits until after the first two trials.
